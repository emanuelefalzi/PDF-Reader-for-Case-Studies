4.1 The notion of obligation
Contents: 4.1.1 The elements of the relationship — 4.1.2 The sources

An obligation is the legal relationship by which a specific person, called the debtor, is bound to a specific financial performance in favor of another person, called the creditor, who is entitled to demand it. The bond does not fall on the person of the debtor but on their assets: in this, current law departs from the obligatio of archaic Roman law.

4.1.1 The elements of the relationship

The relationship has three essential elements: the parties, the performance and the legal bond. The performance must be possible, lawful, determined or determinable, and capable of economic valuation art. 1174 Civil Code; it must also correspond to an interest, even a non-financial one, of the creditor.

4.1.2 The sources

Art. 1173 Civil Code identifies the sources of obligations as contract, tort and any other act or fact capable of giving rise to one in accordance with the legal system. The formula, adopted by the Civil Code of 1942, goes beyond the threefold division handed down by Gaius and taken up by Justinian's Institutiones.

Why is it said that the bond falls on assets and not on the person?
Under current law the creditor cannot act against the person of the debtor: if the performance is not carried out, protection goes through the debtor's assets. In archaic Roman law, by contrast, the obligatio bound the body.
